## 1. Questions I would ask Registry first

Before building anything, I would want to sit down with Registry and understand how registration works today.

In particular:

- How do students register? Is it self-service, done by their school, or entered by Registry staff? And who is allowed to change a registration after that?
- What credit load should part-time students have? I assumed 60, but I'd want to confirm.
- Are students allowed to take modules from other subjects? One Marine Biology student is on a History module, and I couldn't tell if that was allowed.
- When a student withdraws, what is supposed to happen to their module registrations, and whose job is it?
- Who sets module capacity?
- Is there a cut-off date for changes before the census, and how are late changes handled?

## 2. Where the problems could be stopped earlier

Looking at the journey from a student registering to the census return:

    Modules set up  →  Student registers  →  Changes and withdrawals  →  Nightly export  →  Census return

Most of the problems I found come from the registration step.

- Wrong, mistyped or non-running module codes could be avoided if students picked from a list of this year's running modules instead of typing a code. This would
  need the schools (who set up the modules) and Digital Services.
- Duplicate registrations could be blocked by the system itself, so the same module can't be added twice for one student.
- Full modules could be closed or put on a waiting list once they reach capacity. Also limits needs to looked into.
- Wrong credit totals could be caught while the student is registering, by showing their running total and not letting them submit until it adds up. 
- Withdrawn students still being registered is a process gap. When a withdrawal is completed, their registrations should end automatically.
- Changes after the census date should need Registry's approval, so the return and the records don't drift apart without anyone knowing.
- The date format problem is in the export itself, so Digital Services can fix that at source.

## 3. What I would do first

The quickest win is to run the checker every night from the start of registration, not just once at census. Each team gets its own list, so problems are fixed weeks ahead instead of at the last minute. It needs no system changes, and after a few weeks we would know which problems happen most often. Next, I would fix the withdrawal process so registrations end automatically. It's one process owned by one team, and it directly affects the accuracy of the return. After that, I would look at adding validation at the registration step. This would prevent most of the problems at source, but it needs input from the different teams, so I would plan it as a small project ahead of next year's registration.
